## Who we are

We are **Atlanta's on-ramp to practical AI for working professionals.** Not a
research lab, not a builders' guild, not a vendor pitch series — a trusted,
in-person community where a marketer, a lawyer, an HR lead, or a financial advisor
can go from "AI-curious and a little anxious" to "I used this on real work this
week."

Three things define us:

- **In-person and validated.** We show up in a room, in Atlanta, on a cadence people
  can plan around (2nd Wednesday, Improving–Alpharetta). Our calendar only lists
  events we can stand behind — real address, real organizer, real value. Trust is the
  product.
- **Fundamentals-first, role-specific.** We meet professionals where their job is.
  "AI Fundamentals for Professionals" isn't generic — it's tailored to the 17+
  audiences we serve (Sales, HR, Legal, Real Estate, Marketing, Healthcare,
  Nonprofit, and more). The magic is the *transfer to their actual work*, not the
  tool tour.
- **Local chapter of a global movement.** We carry the credibility and playbook of
  The AI Collective (250+ chapters, 60+ countries) but our value is hyper-local:
  the people you can shake hands with and follow up with over coffee.

*One-liner draft:* **"Where Atlanta professionals learn to actually use AI — in
person, in plain language, on real work."**

## Who we are *not* for

Naming this protects the room. We are **not** for:

- **Deep-tech builders and ML researchers** looking for GPU talk, model internals, or
  a hackathon-only scene. They're better served elsewhere (and we should know who to
  refer them to — that's a feature, not a gap).
- **People looking for a lead-gen list or a stage to sell from.** Vendors and
  recruiters are welcome as *members and contributors*, not as a captive audience.
  The moment a session feels like a pitch, we've lost.
- **Pure online / passive learners.** There's infinite free AI content on YouTube.
  If someone only wants to watch a webinar in sweatpants, we're not the differentiated
  choice — the in-person, peer, "I tried it and got stuck, help me" energy is.
- **The already-fluent power users** who want an advanced/edge community. We can grow
  a track for them later, but they are not who "Fundamentals" is built to serve, and
  pretending otherwise dilutes the beginner's welcome.

*Open question for the group:* do we intentionally build a second, more advanced
track over time — or stay ruthlessly focused on the on-ramp and refer power users
out? I lean **stay focused for now**; the on-ramp is underserved and it's our edge.

## Who do we serve

- **Mid-career professionals in non-technical roles** who know AI matters to their
  career but haven't crossed the "I use it confidently at work" line. The anxious
  achiever is our core persona.
- **Team leads and managers** who need to bring AI to their people and want a safe,
  credible place to learn *and* to send their team.
- **Small-business owners, solo practitioners, and consultants** (agents, advisors,
  attorneys, marketers) for whom AI fluency is now a competitive moat.
- **Career-transitioners and returners** who want a supportive room to skill up
  without being the least-technical person in a builders' meetup.
- **Our own chapter leaders and facilitators** — serving them well (credibility,
  ready-to-run material, prep tooling) is how everything else scales.

*Common thread:* they want **applied confidence**, delivered by peers they trust, in a
room they can return to monthly. Belonging is as much the product as the content.

## Who don't we serve

- **Anyone chasing hype over usefulness.** If the goal is buzzwords or a certificate
  to wave around, we're the wrong room.
- **Extractors** — people whose primary aim is to harvest the audience (sell,
  recruit, farm emails) rather than contribute.
- **Those unwilling to do the work.** We're hands-on. If you won't open the tool and
  try it, the value doesn't land — and that's okay, it's just not us.
- **Markets/audiences we can't yet serve with quality.** Better to run one great
  Atlanta session than five thin ones we can't stand behind. Saying "not yet" to
  expansion is a form of respect for the standard.

## How do we serve them?

1. **A dependable monthly ritual.** Same cadence, same welcoming room, predictable
   quality. Reliability compounds trust; trust compounds attendance.
2. **Role-tailored, hands-on sessions.** Not "here's ChatGPT" but "here's how *you*,
   in *your* role, ship better/faster this week." Audience-specific ICPs, real tools,
   take-home handouts, and a `me.md`-style context file so their AI tools answer as
   *them*.
3. **Credible, relatable facilitation.** Leaders who've done the work say plainly why
   they're qualified to lead each topic — peer, not guru. Credibility you can vouch
   for beats celebrity.
4. **A curated, no-noise calendar.** We do the filtering so members don't have to —
   only validated, in-person, genuinely-AI events. We are their trusted signal in a
   sea of noise.
5. **Warm follow-through, not one-and-done.** The room is the start of a relationship:
   newsletter, next event, intros between members, a Slack/community thread to get
   unstuck between sessions.
6. **Free/low-friction on-ramp, deeper paid paths for the ready.** In-person AIC
   sessions stay accessible; those who want to go deeper have a next step (e.g.
   Stitchs / Maven cohorts) — the community feeds the ladder, never the reverse.
7. **Tools that make leaders effective.** We invest in facilitator tooling (workshop
   builder, calendar, newsletter builder) so quality is repeatable and expansion
   doesn't mean dilution.
